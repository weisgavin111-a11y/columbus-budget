# Civic explainer style

The reader is a Columbus resident with no accounting background who wants to understand where public money goes. Write the way a patient accounting TA explains a concept to a first-year student: concrete, short, never condescending.

## Structure of every section
1. **A question a resident would actually ask** as the heading. ("Did the city spend more than it took in?", not "Fund Balance Analysis")
2. **A one-sentence answer** with the key number bolded. Answer the question first.
3. **One chart**, sorted largest to smallest, labeled with dollars and percent of total.
4. **One or two sentences of context** explaining a rule or mechanism behind the number (e.g., the 75/25 income tax split). Context comes from a cited source, never from general knowledge.

## Neutrality rules
- Describe, don't judge. Allowed: "Public safety was 67% of spending." Not allowed: "Public safety dominates the budget," "only," "just," "a whopping," "bloated," "starved."
- Don't attribute motives to officials. Name an official only when a document names them as its author or when quoting a document's own words.
- When a figure is politically contested (overtime, staffing, cuts council restored), present the documented numbers from each official source side by side and say where they differ. Do not pick a side.
- Comparisons over time are fine; frame them as change ("up 4% from 2025"), not as good or bad.
- No predictions.

## Numbers
- Round in prose ($918.2M, 76.6%); keep exact figures in the data file.
- Always say which fund and which basis. Never compare budgetary-basis and GAAP figures.
- Percentages must say "of what": "76.6% of general fund revenue."
- If a number came from a news article rather than a city document, don't use it. Find the primary source.

## Words to define on first use
General fund, encumbrance, budgetary basis, fund balance, transfer, ACFR, OPEB, net pension liability. Add each to the page's "Terms" section.

## Before handing back copy
Re-read every sentence and ask: could a reader tell which side the writer is on? If yes, rewrite it.
